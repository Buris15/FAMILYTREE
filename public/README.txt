LACSAMANA × ORTEGA FAMILY TREE
=================================

PROJECT FILES
-------------
index.html   Page layout, navigation, cousin finder, profile drawer, and sources.
styles.css   Black visual theme, circular family nodes, connectors, and responsive styling.
script.js    Family records, relationship calculations, tree rendering, interactions, and local edits.

OPEN THE SITE
-------------
1. Extract the ZIP file into a folder.
2. Keep index.html, styles.css, and script.js together in that folder.
3. Open index.html in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari.
4. For development, open the folder in VS Code and use Live Server.

USE THE TREE
------------
- Click or tap a circular person profile to open the information drawer.
- The drawer lists recorded parents, spouse/partner, children, sisters, brothers, and cousins.
- Use the “Cousins: Off” button to turn cousin highlighting and the named cousin list on or off.
- Select any person in the cousin finder to list their 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, and further cousins. Exact names appear beneath each degree heading. The relationship wording includes removals where generations differ, for example “1st cousin once removed of [selected person's name]”.
- Click a cousin's name to locate them in the tree.
- Drag the tree to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use Fit tree to reset the view.
- The branch buttons jump to the shared ancestors, Francisca's descendants, Leon's descendants, separate research records, or sources.

RELATIONSHIP LOGIC
------------------
Siblings and cousin degrees are calculated from the parent-child connections recorded in script.js. Cousins are not labelled by vague family branch names; each result gives the person's full name and the exact relationship to the selected individual. A result is marked provisional when the path includes a parent link still needing confirmation. Marriage partners are listed separately from blood relatives.

EDITING AND BACKUPS
--------------------
The site supports local add/edit actions from person profiles. Those changes are stored in the current browser only. Use Export edits to save a JSON backup and Import edits to move a backup to another device. Keep a copy of the original files before making code changes.

Spouse links are short horizontal connectors; parent-child links are drawn from the recorded tree structure. The layout is responsive and supports pointer dragging, touch panning/pinch zoom, and keyboard activation of profile buttons.

SURNAME HISTORY & CULTURAL REFERENCE
------------------------------------
The page now includes a separate “Name history” section. FamilySearch's surname entry, citing the Dictionary of American Family Names, describes Lacsamana as a Filipino surname from a Hispanicized form of Lakshmana, Rama's younger brother in the Ramayana. The section also links Juan R. Francisco's discussion of Malay laksamana as an admiral title derived from Lakshmana, and the Malay Language Centre (Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka) dictionary definition. These are linguistic and cultural explanations; they do not prove that this specific Lacsamana family descends from the epic character, has a particular South Asian ancestry, or included naval officers. The exact historical path by which the surname entered the Bangar–La Union family remains a research question.

The same section mentions Nicole G. Lacsamana, a fictional character listed in the community-maintained Until Then Wiki. The page is marked as a stub. Nicole is kept outside the genealogy and is not presented as a relative of Don Leon or Doña Francisca. The Steam listing identifies Until Then as a game developed by Polychroma Games and released June 25, 2024.

LOCAL ARCHIVE ENTRIES
---------------------
Stories, biography notes, property leads, and source links entered into the new forms are saved in this browser's localStorage. The “Export archive notes” button downloads these text entries as JSON. This is separate from the existing family-tree edits export.

Uploaded document / photograph files are stored in this browser's IndexedDB, separately from the ZIP. They do not automatically transfer to another browser or computer, and they are not included in the archive-notes JSON export. Keep the original files and backups in a safe location. A file upload is limited to 20 MB. The two included FamilySearch screenshots are packaged in the assets folder and remain available whenever the project folder is kept together.

EVIDENCE HANDLING
-----------------
The timeline, place index, biographies, and archive examples reuse information already present in the previous project and user-supplied research. Labels distinguish FamilySearch screenshots, published family-history material, archival register references, indexed civil-registration summaries, and family-reported memories. A birthplace or residence does not prove nationality or property ownership. The map is an index of places mentioned in records; it does not assert an undocumented migration route. No new identity, property ownership, or family relationship is inferred by these archive sections.

Keep index.html, styles.css, script.js, and the assets folder together. The project remains a plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript site; the OpenStreetMap iframe needs internet access, while the rest of the tree and archive sections work locally.

VERSION 10 — SPANISH–PHILIPPINE FAMILY HERITAGE FLAGS
----------------------------------------------------
The family tree now displays heritage flags beside profile names where the current parent-child data connects the person to Evaristo Guadalupe y Villaverde:
- Evaristo is marked with 🇪🇸 because the FamilySearch screenshot and Augustinian archival register record his birthplace as Ocaña, Toledo, Spain.
- People descended from Don Leon or Doña Francisca are marked 🇵🇭 🇪🇸 to represent Philippine family roots and the Spanish-born ancestral line through Evaristo.
- Joaquin Ortega is marked 🇵🇭 based on the existing family-history biography that describes him as Filipino.

IMPORTANT: The flags describe ancestral origin / family heritage, not present-day citizenship or a legal nationality determination. They do not mean that every descendant is exactly half Spanish. The tree calculates the theoretical pedigree contribution from Evaristo's single ancestral path as 50% for his children, 25% for grandchildren, 12.5% for great-grandchildren, 6.25% for the next generation, and so on. This is a pedigree calculation, not a DNA test or a complete ethnicity estimate; other ancestral branches may add further Spanish ancestry. The parentage links from Evaristo to Leon and Francisca are based on the supplied FamilySearch tree screenshots and remain marked as needing corroboration from original civil or church records.

The heritage explanation and sources are included in the Name & Cultural History section and in the profile drawer for each flagged person. The historical legal context is drawn from the U.S. Department of State's 1873 transcription of the Spanish Constitution of 23 May 1845 and the Royal Decree of 17 November 1852. The Constitution text stated that persons born within Spain's dominions were Spaniards and that children of a Spanish father or mother could be Spanish subjects even when born outside those dominions. This historical context is not a ruling on current Spanish citizenship for descendants.
Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1873p1v2/d217
Augustinian archival register: https://www.agustinosvalladolid.es/estudio/investigacion/archivoagustiniano/archivofondos/archivo2019/archivo_2019.pdf

V15 ADDITIONS — FAMILY CONTRIBUTIONS AND DIGITAL LEGACY
-------------------------------------------------------
The Family Contribution Portal accepts submissions only from invited Supabase family-contributor accounts or administrators. Public visitors cannot submit. Contributors can submit missing-relative leads, relationship corrections, record references, photographs, documents, family stories, oral histories, migration/place stories, achievements, and research leads.

Every submission is private and pending until an administrator reviews it. Admins can approve privately, approve and publish (only if the contributor requested public display), or reject. Approved contributions do not automatically alter the official family tree; an administrator must verify the evidence and make any person/relationship changes separately.

To activate V15 contributions on an existing V14 Supabase project, run `supabase/migrations/002_family_contributions.sql` in Supabase Dashboard > SQL Editor. Then invite relatives in Authentication > Users and add each invited user's UUID to `public.family_contributors` using the SQL described in README-DEPLOY.md. The existing administrator account remains in `public.family_admins`.

The Digital Family Museum presents curated exhibits from supplied images and current family research. Family migration/place stories organize already-recorded geography events and administrator-sourced narratives; a sequence of places is not automatically a proven travel route. The existing Stories & Oral History section remains the place for preserved family interviews. The Legacy Book Generator can now include migration stories, museum exhibits, and approved public contributions.

Contribution attachments use a private Supabase Storage bucket and signed, time-limited review URLs. They are not publicly exposed. Only the text and source information from contributions that an administrator explicitly approves for publication are displayed publicly.
